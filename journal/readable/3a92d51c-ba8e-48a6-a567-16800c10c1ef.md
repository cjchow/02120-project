**Today's idea: the faucet, `ABSORBED`** ([main.py:19](project/folder2/main.py#L19))

```python
ABSORBED = S0 / 4 * (1 - ALBEDO)   # ~238 W/m^2
```

1. **`S0 = 1361`** is the sunlight hitting the top of the atmosphere, measured in **W/m²** (watts per square meter, meaning how much energy lands on each square meter every second).
2. **`/ 4`** is there because Earth is a ball. It catches sunlight like a flat disk, but the ball has **4 times** the area of that disk, and half of it is in night at any moment. Dividing by 4 spreads the sunlight evenly over the whole globe.
3. **`(1 - ALBEDO)`**: **albedo** is the fraction of sunlight that bounces straight back to space, here 30%. So 70% is kept.
4. In the time loop, this number gets multiplied by `trans[i]` ([main.py:54](project/folder2/main.py#L54)). That's the haze turning the faucet down.

### What you learned today

**Recap of lifetime**
- After 2 lifetimes, about 13.5% (e^(−2)) of the haze is left. It's the same math as sunlight getting through at τ = 2.
- Long-term cooling depends more on a **long lifetime** (sulfate) than on a big starting tau (soot).

**The faucet: `ABSORBED`**
- `ABSORBED = (S0 / 4) * (1 - ALBEDO)` ≈ **238 W/m²**, the sunlight the surface takes in under a clear sky.
- **W/m²** is energy per second landing on each square meter. It's an amount, not a percentage.
- **/4:** Earth catches sunlight like a disk but has 4× that area as a sphere, so the sunlight gets spread over the whole globe.
- **Albedo** is the fraction reflected straight back to space (30%). So 70% is kept.
- Python evaluates `/` and `*` left to right, so only the 4 is in the denominator.

**How the haze turns the faucet down**
- Incoming sunlight = `ABSORBED × trans`, where `trans = e^(−τ)` ([main.py:48](project/folder2/main.py#L48), [main.py:54](project/folder2/main.py#L54)).
- τ = 0 gives the full 238. τ = 2 gives about 32 W/m², so the surface cools.
