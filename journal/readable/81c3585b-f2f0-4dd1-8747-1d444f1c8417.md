**Where this fits:** optical depth is **step 1** of your model's chain. It's the number that decides how far the faucet gets turned down. Everything after it (temperature, photosynthesis, food web) depends on it.

**Idea 1: what optical depth is**

1. **Optical depth** (written **τ**, the Greek letter "tau") is a single number that says **how thick the haze is** between the Sun and the ground.
2. **τ = 0** means a perfectly clear sky, so all of the sunlight gets through.
3. A **bigger τ** means more soot or sulfate is in the way, so less sunlight reaches the ground.
4. An analogy: think of τ as **how many sheets of tissue paper** you're holding up in front of a flashlight. Each sheet blocks some of the light.

In your code, τ comes from the function `optical_depth()` at [main.py:28](project/folder2/main.py#L28). It adds up the soot's τ and the sulfate's τ.

**Idea 2: each layer lets through the same fraction**

1. Go back to the tissue paper. Say each sheet lets **half** of the light that reaches it pass through.
2. With one sheet, half the light gets through: **50%**.
3. The second sheet doesn't see the full flashlight. It only sees the 50% that made it past the first sheet, and it lets **half of that** through.
4. So stacking layers **multiplies** the fractions. It doesn't subtract them.

**Idea 3: that multiplying rule *is* `exp(-tau)`**

1. `exp(-tau)` means *e*^(−τ), where *e* ≈ 2.718. It's a shorthand for "multiply by 0.368 once for every 1 unit of τ."
2. So e^(−1) = 0.368, e^(−2) = 0.368 × 0.368, e^(−3) = 0.368 × 0.368 × 0.368, and so on. That's exactly what you just did by hand.
3. This is what **Bardeen** means when they write that sunlight "declines **exponentially** with optical depth." **Exponentially** means you multiply by the same fraction for every step, instead of subtracting the same amount.
4. In your code, line 46 ([main.py:46](project/folder2/main.py#L46)) does this for every day: `trans = np.exp(-optical_depth(...))`. Here `trans` is the **transmission**, the fraction of sunlight that gets through.
