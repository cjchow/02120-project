**Claude:** Mostly right! The optical depth section, the units table, and the whole derivation are solid. There's one real mistake that shows up twice, plus a few wording fixes.

**Real mistakes (these change the meaning)**

1. **"Heat of tank = C_S × 150" and "m×C = C_S × 150":** the 150 is **already inside** C_S. In the code, `C_S = 4.2e6 * 150`. Change both lines to:
   **C_S = 4.2e6 × 150 = (density × specific heat) × depth**
2. **"C_S is 4.2e6 J/m³K":** 4.2e6 is only the density × specific heat part. C_S is the whole thing (6.3e8 J/(m²·K)). Try: "4.2e6 J/(m³·K) = how much heat 1 m³ of water needs to warm 1 K".
3. **"1 m³ water = 1000 g":** it's **1000 kg = 1,000,000 g**. That's where the e6 comes from: 4.18 J/(g·K) × 1,000,000 g/m³ ≈ 4.2e6 J/(m³·K). With 1000 g you'd get only 4,180.
4. **"dt × (3.156e7 s / 1 yr) = dt in years":** it should be **= dt in seconds**. Converting to seconds is the whole point of that step.

**Wording fixes**

5. **Optical depth:** "does NOT say how much sunlight gets through. That is determined with: tau_soot × e^(−t/t_soot)" makes it sound like that equation gives sunlight. It gives **how tau shrinks over time**. Sunlight comes later, from e^(−tau), which your last line already says. Try: "Tau decays over time: tau_soot × e^(−t_yr / t_soot)".
6. **"each run of the model is 1 day":** change "run" to **step**. One run takes many daily steps.
7. **"bc big net is in seconds":** change it to **big net is *per* second** (J/s/m²).
8. **TankB (1000 m):** correct! Add a note that this is the deep ocean's `C_D`. More precisely, it warms slower because it needs more joules for each kelvin.
