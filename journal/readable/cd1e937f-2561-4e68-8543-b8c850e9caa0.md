**Claude:** Good catch. They're related, but your model isn't Lotka-Volterra. Here's the one key idea:

1. **Lotka-Volterra works in both directions.** Prey feed predators, *and* predators eat the prey back down. The prey equation has a minus term (prey × predators) for being eaten. That two-way pull makes the populations cycle up and down.
2. **Your line 67 only works in one direction.** In [main.py:67](project/folder2/main.py#L67), `web[i+1] = web[i] + rates * web[i] * (food - web[i]) * dt`, each level moves toward its food supply. Nothing ever subtracts the effect of the level above it. Ecologists call this a **bottom-up** model: control flows only from food up the chain.
3. **The family resemblance:** if you multiply out line 67 for predators, you get `rate·pred·herb − rate·pred²`. The first term, the (predator × prey) product, is exactly Lotka-Volterra's gain term. It's the same "mass action" idea as rate = k[A][B] in your chemical-reactions class. The difference is the loss term. Lotka-Volterra uses a plain death rate (−γ·pred), and yours uses crowding (−pred²). So the formal name for yours is the logistic tracking we labeled.
4. **Why this matters today:** "Is logistic tracking a reasonable simplification?" is already on your TA list for 3:30. This is a good way to ask it: "I chose bottom-up, not Lotka-Volterra. Is ignoring top-down effects OK for an impact winter, where food collapse is the main driver?"
