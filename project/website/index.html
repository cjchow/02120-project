Reading the graphs

        
The time axis stretches the first year (monthly steps), then counts years on a log scale out to 60. Hover for exact values.

        
The dashed gray line is the no-impact control. It stays flat at 15 °C (288 K), which checks that the model is balanced before the impact.

        
The animals

        
The animals at the bottom are a drawing of the model's herbivore and predator levels. The herd size follows each level's lowest point: a full herd is 8 herbivores and 6 predators (assumed sizes), times the lowest biomass fraction, with at least 1 survivor. The ferns, trees and the green plankton dots in the sea follow the producers level the same way: at its lowest point, missing trees stand dead and missing ferns leave bare ground. The small sea animals (fish, ammonites, belemnites, baby turtles) follow the herbivore level, and the plesiosaur and Mosasaurus follow the predator level (both at full strength, at least 1 survivor). When one animal wanders off, another kind from the same level walks on, so the count stays the same. All five kinds lived at the impact. Quetzalcoatlus is a flying pterosaur and the plesiosaur and Mosasaurus are marine reptiles. None of these three is a dinosaur.

        
This is a simple global-mean model. Its three food-web levels are generic groups, not counts of any species.

        
Graphs show output of run() (exported by export_data.py to data.json) with soot τ = 8.0, lasting 1.5 yr, and sulfate τ = 2.0, lasting 3.0 yr.
